# What major quant managers and research institutions publish about systematic and AI-driven equity strategies that hold up live

Scope note (researched 2026-10-10): institutional and academic-practitioner evidence on multi-signal stock selection, momentum construction, ML/LLM use, Asia/Taiwan specifics, risk and drawdown control, and strategy evaluation and retirement. Most institutional sources are written by the firms themselves. Where a source sells a related product, or reports a result only in a press release, it is marked **[vendor]** or **[secondary]**. Sharpe = annualized Sharpe ratio unless stated. "Our context" means the project: a single user, long-only, 20-stock rules on Taiwan stocks, daily data, 17 "T0" candidates from a large factor-combination scan (signals include 52-week high, RSI, MACD, margin growth, EPS growth, intraday momentum and GBDT scores), 20–40 orders a month, with a same-cash-flow 0050 benchmark that is never held.

## 1. Signal combination: integrated vs mixed, weighting, factor timing, factor momentum, crowding, alpha decay and capacity, and how many signals to combine

### Takeaway
In practice the institutions combine many weakly correlated signals into **one composite score per stock** (integrated), weight signals roughly equally or with heavy shrinkage, and avoid valuation-based factor timing. They treat crowding and post-publication decay as real but modest. AQR's evidence favours integration for long-only portfolios, but an academic replication finds the edge is not significant after multiple-testing correction. The robust messages are to diversify across signal families and to keep the weighting simple.

### Cited Findings
**Integrated vs mixed (long-only)**
- AQR (Fitzgibbons, Friedman, Pomorski, Serban, "Long-Only Style Investing: Don't Just Mix, Integrate") builds one combined style score per stock instead of mixing single-style sleeves. The integrated version raised excess returns by about 1% a year and the information ratio by about 40% versus mixed portfolios, and the advantage grew as more factors were combined — [ai-CIO summary](https://www.ai-cio.com/news/factor-investing-allocation-vs-integration/); [AQR abstract page](https://www.aqr.com/Insights/Research/White-Papers/Long-Only-Style-Investing) **[vendor]**.
- Alpha Architect's reading of the AQR chart: at matched tracking error, the mixed ("silo") portfolio earned about 1.4% alpha over MSCI World and the integrated one about 3.2%. The mechanism is that value and momentum are negatively correlated, so an integrated score selects stocks with "reasonably positive exposure to many styles" rather than extremes on one. Mixing still gives cleaner attribution, and concentration-hungry factors such as value and momentum suit it — [Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/should-investors-combine-or-separate-their-factor-exposures/).
- Counter-evidence: an academic study ("The mixed vs the integrated approach to style investing: Much ado about nothing?") finds integration superior in only a few style combinations, and after adjusting for multiple hypothesis testing the two approaches cannot be distinguished — [vLex abstract](https://eu.vlex.com/vid/the-mixed-vs-the-855646895).
- Newfound Research objects that integration ignores the different horizons over which factors "mature". The integrated portfolio therefore ends up driven more by the higher-turnover factors — [Newfound Research](https://blog.thinknewfound.com/2016/07/).

**Weighting: equal vs optimized vs ML**
- DeMiguel, Garlappi and Uppal (RFS 2009) tested 14 optimizing models on 7 datasets, and none consistently beat 1/N on Sharpe ratio, certainty-equivalent return or turnover. With US-calibrated parameters, sample mean-variance needs an estimation window of about 3,000 months to beat 1/N with 25 assets and about 6,000 months with 50 assets. The authors' explanation is that the estimation error in the optimizer's inputs costs more than optimal diversification gains — [LBS](https://london.edu/faculty-and-research/academic-research/o/optimal-versus-naive-diversification-how-inefficient-is-the-1n-portfolio-strategy-p7805); [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/rfinst/v22y2009i5p1915-1953.html). A 2019 FAJ rebuttal ("In Defense of Portfolio Optimization") argues mean-variance does at least as well as 1/N once there is modest forecasting skill — [CFA Institute](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/research/financial-analysts-journal/2019/ip-in-defense-of-portfolio-optimization).
- Rasekhschaffe and Jones (FAJ 2019, "Machine Learning for Stock Selection") use two main tools against overfitting. One is **forecast combination**: averaging forecasts from different algorithm types, each trained on smaller or different training sets. The other is **feature engineering** — [CFA Institute summary](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/research/financial-analysts-journal/2019/ip-machine-learning-for-stock-selection). A secondary write-up reports that a composite of 12 signals × 4 algorithms × 3 training sets earned 1.61% a month of net four-factor alpha (equal-weighted) across 22 developed markets, 1994–2016 — [CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/investing-expertise/machine-learning-factor/) **[secondary; not checked against tables]**.
- Robeco's EM machine-learning study found that ensembling all its ML models could raise expected net returns by up to 2% a year — [Robeco](https://www.robeco.com/en-us/insights/2023/10/using-machine-learning-for-emerging-market-equity-returns) **[vendor]**.

**Factor timing (mostly negative)**
- Asness, Chandra, Ilmanen and Israel (AQR, JPM 2017, "Contrarian Factor Timing Is Deceptively Difficult") find that value-spread timing of styles looks promising on its own. Inside a multi-style portfolio that already holds value, though, the results are "somewhat disappointing". Simulations showed no meaningful gross improvement, and timing becomes "progressively harder" to add value as the base portfolio diversifies. The authors call it "a weak addition" for diversified investors — [Capital Spectator summary](https://capitalspectator.com/?p=8630); [CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/value-premium/valuation-based-factor-timing/). The paper was partly a rebuttal of Research Affiliates' contrarian factor-timing papers — [Institutional Investor](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/b1505qjg9dyd03/cliff-asness-blasts-rob-arnott-on-factor-timing).
- Morningstar (Alex Bryan) independently judged the valuation–factor-return link "probably modest at best". He attributed much of it to the 1999–2000 extremes — [Morningstar](https://tw.morningstar.com/tw/news/171455/to-time-or-not-to-time-(part-2).aspx).
- Asness's stated rule is that timing helps only at extremes. The extreme case is declaring a factor permanently dead, which should rest on a broken rationale rather than a bad run — [Quantpedia on Asness](https://quantpedia.com/cliff-asnesss-aqr-view-on-factor-timing/); [ai-CIO](https://www.ai-cio.com/news/dont-try-to-time-factors-says-cliff-asness/).

**Factor momentum (the one timing signal with broad support)**
- Gupta and Kelly (AQR/Yale, "Factor Momentum Everywhere") studied 65 US and 62 global long-short factors from the mid-1960s. Time-series factor momentum was positive for 59 of the 65, and significant for 49. Averaging the one-month signal across all factors gave a Sharpe of 0.84, higher than any single factor's. The effect is "equally strong" outside the US — [CXO Advisory](https://cxoadvisory.com/momentum-investing/ubiquitous-equity-factor-momentum); [Institutional Investor](https://institutionalinvestor.com/article/b1cmczbjjl4qgs/Factor-Momentum-Is-Real-Researchers-Argue) **[secondary figures]**.
- Ehsani and Linnainmaa (JF 2022) find that stock momentum largely aggregates the autocorrelation of other factors. Factors earned about 6 bp a month after a losing year and 51 bp a month after a winning year — [NBER w25551](https://www.nber.org/papers/w25551).
- Taiwan evidence (NTU master's thesis, 2023, 27 factors): most annual financial-statement factors show positive 12-month factor momentum, but most monthly trading-based factors show **negative** autocorrelation. The significant cases are mainly profitability-related (OCF/assets, cash-flow-to-price, quality) — [NTU thesis](https://tdr.lib.ntu.edu.tw/handle/123456789/88739) **[thesis, not peer-reviewed]**.

**Crowding**
- MSCI's Integrated Factor Crowding Model (Bonne, Roisenberg, Kouzmenko, Zangari, June 2018) combines five metrics: valuation spread, short-interest spread, pairwise correlation inside quintiles, factor volatility relative to market volatility, and 36-month factor reversal. They are combined into a z-score. Over 1996–2018, scores ≥ 1 were followed by significantly more frequent large drawdowns over 12 months. Momentum scored about 1.2 in August 2018 and drew down sharply in early 2019 — [MSCI blog](https://www.msci.com/research-and-insights/blog-post/where-were-the-factor-crowds-this-summer); [MSCI "Back-to-school momentum blues"](https://www.msci.com/research-and-insights/blog-post/back-to-school-momentum-blues) **[vendor; few episodes behind the threshold]**.
- Research Affiliates lists "How crowded is this factor?" as one of three questions an investor must ask before trusting a backtest — [Research Affiliates, "Alice's Adventures in Factorland" (2019)](https://researchaffiliates.com/publications/articles/710-alices-adventures-in-factorland-three-blunders-that-plague-factor-investing).

**Alpha decay and capacity**
- McLean and Pontiff (JF 2016, 97 predictors): portfolio returns are 26% lower out-of-sample (an upper bound on data mining) and 58% lower after publication. Decay is larger for predictors with higher in-sample returns, and returns are higher in high-idiosyncratic-risk, low-liquidity stocks — [paper PDF](https://Www.Gwern.net/doc/economics/2016-mclean.pdf); [Ivey working version](https://ivey.uwo.ca/media/3775549/pontiff.pdf).
- Frazzini, Israel and Moskowitz (AQR) use about $1 trillion of AQR's own live trades (1998–2011, 19 markets), later about $1.7 trillion over 21 markets. Actual trading costs were less than a tenth of earlier academic estimates. Break-even fund sizes in the US were about $103bn for size, $83bn for value and $52bn for momentum (global: $156bn, $190bn and $89bn). Short-term reversal stops surviving costs above about $9bn in the US — [Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/surprise-the-size-value-and-momentum-anomalies-survive-after-trading-costs/); [AQR working paper page](https://www.aqr.com/Insights/Research/Working-Paper/Trading-Costs-of-Asset-Pricing-Anomalies) **[vendor's own execution]**.
- Contrast: Brière, Lehalle, Nefedova and Raboun use broad US institutional trade data. They estimate average annual costs of 17 bp for size, 24 bp for value and **274 bp for momentum**, with momentum capacity of only about $310–410m — [Institut Louis Bachelier](https://institutlouisbachelier.org/en/ilb-publications-en/hal-02283349). The gap between the two shows that costs depend on execution skill.

**How many signals, and decorrelation**
- WorldQuant's "101 Formulaic Alphas" (Kakushadze, 2016) are 101 real production alphas with average holding periods of 0.6–6.4 days. Their **average pairwise correlation is 15.9%**. Returns scale with volatility, not turnover, and 80 of the 101 were still in production when the paper was published — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1601.00991); [CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/technical-trading/performance-of-actual-quant-strategies/).
- Renaissance lore: Zuckerman's book quotes Robert Mercer as saying the firm was right about 50.75% of the time, "You can make billions that way." Zuckerman reads this as a slight edge across thousands of simultaneous trades — [reader notes on *The Man Who Solved the Market*](https://www.goodreads.com/notes/46025922-the-man-who-solved-the-market/13424516-zubair-habib/bd279716-8be3-44b0-849d-d5527dbb7cd6) **[book lore, secondary]**. A secondary source puts Medallion's average return at about 66% a year since 1988 — [EBC](https://www.ebc.com/zh-cn/jinrong/38.html) **[unverified]**.
- AQR's fund documents list several momentum measures in one model: price returns, earnings surprises, residual (risk-adjusted) momentum and "indirect" momentum of economically linked firms. They also state that industry neutrality improves risk-adjusted returns across many stock-selection signals — [Mutual Fund Observer on AQR QMNIX, 2016](https://mutualfundobserver.com/2016/04/aqr-equity-market-neutral-qmnix-aqr-long-short-equity-qleix/) **[vendor]**.

### Inferences
- The 17 T0 candidates are effectively a "mixed" book if each is run as its own 20-stock sleeve. The institutional default for a single long-only account is one integrated composite rank, an equal-weighted average of standardized signal ranks, with the signals grouped into **families**. That is not proven better in every combination, but it is cheaper and lowers turnover through netting. The candidate signals cluster into at most four families: (a) price trend, made up of 52-week high, MACD, RSI and intraday momentum, which are likely highly correlated; (b) retail leverage and sentiment (margin growth); (c) fundamentals (EPS growth); and (d) model scores (GBDT, which probably reloads on (a)). Equal weight across families, not across individual signals, prevents the price-trend family from dominating by count.
- Taiwan has about 11 years of data under the project's 2015-06+ design, roughly 135 months. That is far below the DeMiguel-type windows needed for optimized or ML signal weights to beat equal weights. Fixed equal or rank weights are the defensible default, and learned weights should face a large shrinkage prior.
- Valuation-based factor timing should be skipped. If any timing is used, the only family with broad support is slow factor momentum, and the Taiwan thesis suggests it applies to fundamental factors, not to trading-based technical factors.
- Expect live returns well below the backtest. McLean–Pontiff-type decay of 26–58% applies even before the project's own multiple-testing burden from the 39,676-candidate scan.

### Gaps
- I could not open the full AQR "Don't Just Mix, Integrate" paper; the 1%/40% and 1.4%/3.2% figures come from summaries. AQR's page and Alpha Architect also disagree on the journal (JPM vs Journal of Investing).
- I found no Goldman Sachs or BofA quant research on signal weighting or crowding that was accessible and dated. Their published views remain uncovered.
- No institutional source states a "right number" of signals for a 20-stock long-only book.

## 2. Momentum done right: residual and idiosyncratic momentum, quality and path filters, crash management, industry neutrality, 52-week high, and Asia

### Takeaway
The institutional consensus is to keep momentum and build it better. Use residual (idiosyncratic) momentum or a composite of residual momentum, analyst revisions and news. Neutralize industry and market bets. Scale or dampen exposure when momentum's own volatility spikes, especially in rebounds after bear markets. Rebalance no faster than weekly. The 52-week-high variant has unusually good evidence in Taiwan. Plain price momentum is weak in East Asia, which strengthens the case for the residual and 52-week-high versions.

### Cited Findings
- AQR's "Fact, Fiction and Momentum Investing" (Asness, Frazzini, Israel, Moskowitz; JPM 2014) makes several points. US winners minus losers averaged 8.3% a year over 1927–2013. The long and short sides are about equally profitable. Momentum works in large caps nearly as well as in small caps. Institutional trading costs (from AQR's more than $1 trillion of trades) do not kill it. Using momentum only as a **screen** is "significantly suboptimal" compared with using it as a factor — [AQR PDF](https://www.Aqr.Com/-/media/AQR/Documents/Journal-Articles/JPM-Fact-Fiction-and-Momentum-Investing.pdf?sc_lang=en); [ThinkAdvisor](https://thinkadvisor.com/2014/05/13/10-myths-about-momentum-investing-aqr-capital) **[vendor]**.
- Residual and idiosyncratic momentum (Robeco): Blitz, Hanauer and Vidojevic, "The Idiosyncratic Momentum Anomaly" (SSRN 2017, revised 2020), rank stocks on 12-month residuals from a Fama-French three-factor regression. The effect is not explained by standard factors, even including total-return momentum. Crash risk and overreaction do not explain it, which points to underreaction. It is robust across developed and emerging markets — [SSRN author page](https://papers.ssrn.com/author=3570942); [Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/swedroe-spotlight-enhancing-momentum-strategies-via-idiosyncratic-momentum/). CXO Advisory reports a Sharpe of 0.48 for idiosyncratic momentum versus 0.25 for conventional momentum in US data, 1925–2015 — [CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/momentum-investing/idiosyncratic-pure-or-residual-momentum-as-a-stock-return-predictor) **[secondary; units as reported by CXO]**.
- Robeco says its proprietary residualization is applied to the momentum factor in **all** of its quant stock-selection models. Its argument is that residual momentum is driven by underreaction and so is less prone to reversal — [Robeco 2013](https://www.robeco.com/jp-jp/insights/2013/10/robecos-residual-momentum-less-risky-and-more-sustainable) **[vendor]**. In corporate bonds, the residual version roughly halved volatility — [Robeco 2012](https://robeco.com/en-int/insights/2012/08/residual-equity-momentum-for-corporate-bonds).
- Robeco's "Quant chart: taming momentum crashes" (Hanauer, Blitz, Honarvar; Feb 2023) compares two constructions. One is an alternative momentum composite of 12-1 residual momentum, 3-month EPS FY1 revisions and 6-month news sentiment. The other is plain 12-1 price momentum. The test universe is MSCI Developed, equal-weighted, monthly, region- and sector-neutral, over 2001–2023. The composite matched price momentum's returns, slightly higher in the long run, with **shallower drawdowns**, including in the Nov 2022 and Jan–Feb 2023 reversals — [Robeco](https://www.robeco.com/en-latam/insights/2023/02/quant-chart-taming-momentum-crashes) **[vendor]**. Robeco did not publish a product called "enhanced momentum"; this composite is the closest match.
- Momentum crashes: Daniel and Moskowitz ("Momentum Crashes", JFE 2016) find crashes occur in "panic" states, after market declines with high volatility, and coincide with market rebounds. A dynamic strategy based on forecasts of momentum's mean and variance roughly doubles alpha and Sharpe — [NBER w20439](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w20439/w20439.pdf). Barroso and Santa-Clara ("Momentum Has Its Moments", JFE 2015) find momentum risk is predictable. Scaling by 6-month realized variance "virtually eliminates crashes and nearly doubles the Sharpe". Weights ranged from 0.13 to 2.0 with a mean of about 0.9 — [ETF.com (Swedroe)](https://www.etf.com/sections/index-investor-corner/swedroe-downside-momentum). Mechanism in 2009: after 2008, total-return momentum held low-beta names, and the Q1 2009 rebound hit it through that negative beta. Residual momentum carries smaller time-varying factor exposures — [Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/strategies/residual-momentum-factor).
- Path quality ("frog in the pan"): Da, Gurun and Warachka find six-month momentum of 5.94% for stocks whose past returns came in many small moves, versus –2.07% for stocks with lumpy, discrete moves (1927–2007). Alpha Architect's QMOM ranks on 12-2 momentum and then uses frog-in-the-pan as a quality filter for about 50–100 names — [Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/frog-in-the-pan-identifying-the-highest-quality-momentum-stocks/); [Alpha Architect methodology PDF](https://alphaarchitect.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/08/The_Quantitative_Momentum_Investing_Philosophy.pdf) **[vendor]**. Supportive evidence also appears in emerging and non-US developed markets — [IBKR Quant summary](https://www.interactivebrokers.com/campus/ibkr-quant-news/frog-in-the-pan-momentum-international-evidence/).
- Industry neutrality and rebalancing frequency: AQR's "Craftsmanship Alpha" (Israel, Jiang, Ross; JPM) finds that neutralizing industry and market bets usually raises Sharpe through lower volatility, but **raises turnover**. For a simulated momentum portfolio, net performance was **negative with daily rebalancing** and positive at weekly or slower frequencies — [Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/portfolio-craftsmanship-just-important-choosing-investment-style/) **[vendor research; momentum-specific industry-neutral tables not retrieved]**.
- 52-week high: Liu, Liu and Ma (JIMF 2011, 20 countries) find the 52-week-high strategy generally beats conventional momentum and earns abnormal returns in most countries — [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jimfin/v30y2011i1p180-204.html). Gupta, Locke and Scrimgeour (51 countries) find it positive but not larger than conventional momentum — [Waikato](https://researchcommons.waikato.ac.nz/handle/10289/4088). A 2023 Taiwan study cites Chang (2011) and Hao et al. (2016) as showing the 52-week-high strategy is "remarkable" in Taiwan. It also finds that **the updating of the 52-week high itself** drives profits more than the price change does — [IJBESAR Taiwan study](https://ijbesar.ihu.gr/docs/volume16_issue1/16_01_07.pdf). In Robeco's EM ML model, price-to-52-week-high was the **single most important** of 36 features — [Robeco](https://www.robeco.com/en-us/insights/2023/10/using-machine-learning-for-emerging-market-equity-returns).
- Asia: Chui, Titman and Wei (JF 2010) link momentum profits to national individualism scores. East Asian markets score low on individualism and show weak momentum. The authors call the evidence "circumstantial" — [HKUST summary](https://bm.hkust.edu.hk/bizinsight/2010/05/roll-cross-cultural-differences-momentum-profits); [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfinan/v65y2010i1p361-392.html). In China A-shares (Jansen, Swinkels, Zhou; PBFJ 2021; 32 anomalies over 2000–2019), past-return anomalies are weak **except residual momentum and reversal** — [EUR](https://pure.eur.nl/en/publications/anomalies-in-the-china-a-share-market/).
- Short-horizon signals and turnover (Robeco; Blitz, Hanauer, Honarvar, Huisman, van Vliet; FAJ 2023, "Beyond Fama-French Factors: Alpha from Short-Term Signals"): a composite of short-term reversal, short-term momentum, short-term analyst revisions, short-term risk and monthly seasonality has gross alpha above 12% a year. At about 1,000% turnover and 25 bp per one-way trade, costs eat more than two-thirds of it, and single signals go net negative. A **buy-top-10% / hold-until-out-of-top-50%** buffer cut turnover and lifted net alpha above 6%. The signals also work long-only and with a few days' implementation lag — [Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/alpha-from-short-term-signals/); [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/taf/ufajxx/v79y2023i4p96-117.html). Robeco's "enhanced reversal" offsets short-term reversal against short-term industry and factor momentum, which doubles its risk-adjusted return — [Robeco 2023](https://www.robeco.com/en-us/insights/2023/10/reversing-the-trend-of-short-term-reversal) **[vendor]**.
- Live capture is hard: Research Affiliates' 2017 "Alice in Factorland" found that, in fund managers' realized returns, "essentially all of the momentum factor return" disappears relative to paper long-short portfolios — [Research Affiliates video page](https://www.researchaffiliates.com/insights/multimedia/605-alice-in-factorland-and-the-incredible-shrinking-factor-return); [RA PDF](https://originpreprod.researchaffiliates.com/content/dam/ra/publications/pdf/601-the-incredible-shrinking-factor-return.pdf). This contradicts AQR's "trading costs don't eliminate it". The difference is mostly execution skill, as in the AQR vs Brière cost estimates in §1.

### Inferences
- Several of the T0 signals (52-week high, MACD, RSI, intraday momentum) are price-trend measures. The institutional upgrades that map most directly are: (1) residualize returns against the market and industry before computing trend signals, which is cheap to test with daily data; (2) keep the 52-week-high signal, the best-supported price-trend variant in Taiwan; (3) add a "smoothness" filter (frog-in-the-pan) rather than more trend lookbacks; (4) dampen exposure when the momentum portfolio's own trailing volatility is high, or when the market has just rebounded from a deep drawdown.
- RSI, MACD and intraday momentum are short-horizon signals. Robeco's own numbers show these work gross but die at around 1,000% turnover unless a wide buffer is used. Twenty to forty orders a month on a 20-stock book is in that regime (see §5 inference).
- Weak plain momentum in East Asia argues against long-lookback price momentum as a standalone T0. It fits better as one input to a residual/52-week-high/revision composite.

### Gaps
- Two Taiwan claims surfaced in search reviews could not be verified at source: (a) momentum in Taiwan exists only in the months after the annual-report deadline and is contrarian otherwise; (b) Banerjee and Hung find near-zero momentum in Taiwan, Hong Kong and Korea. They are kept here, not in the findings.
- I did not retrieve Dimensional's published treatment of momentum. From background knowledge, unverified here, it uses momentum as a trading screen (delaying buys and sells), which is the approach AQR's myth #6 criticizes.
- I found no published Taiwan-specific test of residual momentum or of industry-neutral momentum.

## 3. Machine learning and LLMs at institutions: what they actually report

### Takeaway
Institutions report that nonlinear ML (gradient-boosted trees, random forests, shallow neural nets, ensembles) beats linear factor models out-of-sample by a modest, real margin. That holds in emerging markets and China too, and the important features are mostly the familiar ones (52-week high, volatility, liquidity, momentum, reversal). Much of the headline ML alpha, however, sits in microcaps and hard-to-trade names and needs high turnover. Model "design choices" add as much noise as sampling error. LLMs are used mainly as research-productivity agents (Man's AlphaGPT) and macro-research aids (Bridgewater), not as direct stock pickers. Published LLM return-prediction alphas suffer look-ahead bias and are decaying as adoption spreads.

### Cited Findings
**Why finance ML is different (AQR)**
- Israel, Kelly and Moskowitz (AQR; JOIM 2020, "Can Machines 'Learn' Finance?", Markowitz Award). Market signal-to-noise is low **by construction**, because competition arbitrages away predictable returns. Data histories are short, and markets evolve. Risk and price impact are much more predictable than returns. Even strong alternative-data signals with short histories should get small weights. Their case study uses 194 characteristics in 22 developed markets over 1994–2016, rerun across 4 models and 3 training windows — [AQR explainer](https://www.aqr.com/Learning-Center/Machine-Learning/Machine-Learning-Why-Finance-Is-Different); [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=3624052).

**Out-of-sample ML evidence**
- Gu, Kelly and Xiu (RFS 2020): trees and neural networks are the best performers, in some cases roughly doubling the performance of leading regression-based strategies, because they capture nonlinear interactions. All methods agree on the dominant predictors: variants of **momentum, liquidity and volatility** — [NBER w25398](https://nber.org/papers/w25398).
- Robeco (Hanauer and Kalsbach, *Emerging Markets Review* 2023) covers more than 15,000 stocks in 32 EM countries over 1990–2021, with 36 standard characteristics. One pooled model is trained on all EM, not country by country, to limit overfitting, with the target being excess return over the country index. In long/short, cap-weighted, out-of-sample tests over 2002–2021, linear models returned about 0.8% a month, trees about 1.0% and neural nets or the ensemble about 1.2%. Alpha after known factors was about 0.2%, 0.5% and 0.7% a month respectively. The top features were **price-to-52-week-high, idiosyncratic volatility and turnover**, with momentum, short-term reversal, P/E and profitability in the top 15. The study reports that results survive costs and short-sale limits but publishes no turnover figure — [Robeco](https://www.robeco.com/en-us/insights/2023/10/using-machine-learning-for-emerging-market-equity-returns); [Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/machine-learning-and-emerging-market-stock-returns/) **[vendor]**.
- Hanauer (Robeco/TUM), "Design Choices, Machine Learning, and the Cross-Section of Stock Returns", fits more than 1,000 ML models. The extra "non-standard error" from researcher design choices is **59% larger than the standard error**, and monthly top-minus-bottom returns range from 0.13% to 1.98% across choices — [Poznań seminar abstract](https://ue.poznan.pl/wydarzenia/17th-online-seminar-in-finance-matthias-hanauer-robeco-technical-university-of-munich).
- Avramov, Cheng and Metzker (Management Science 2023, "Machine Learning vs. Economic Restrictions") find deep-learning payoffs fall about 62% when microcaps are excluded, 68% without non-rated firms and 80% without distressed firms around downgrades. Profits come mainly from hard-to-arbitrage stocks and high limits-to-arbitrage periods. They need high turnover and extreme positions, so "an average investor would struggle to achieve alpha after transaction costs". Positives: the signals are more profitable on the **long side**, remain viable after 2001 and carry low downside risk — [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=3450322); [CUHK Business School](https://cbk.bschool.cuhk.edu.hk/the-limitations-of-using-ai-to-pick-stocks/).
- Leippold, Wang and Zhou (JFE 2022, China A-shares, 1,160 signals): **liquidity** is the most important predictor, unlike in the US. Heavy retail participation raises short-term predictability, especially in small stocks. Large caps and SOEs are more predictable at longer horizons. Fundamentals and liquidity matter more than price-trend signals. Out-of-sample performance stays economically significant after transaction costs — [SFI](https://www.sfi.ch/en/publications/machine-learning-in-the-chinese-stock-market).
- Gradient boosting caution: one review attributes to López de Prado the view that boosting mainly fixes underfitting while bagging (random forests) fixes overfitting, which is the bigger risk in finance. It also notes boosting is sensitive to outliers — [arXiv 2601.13014](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.13014) **[secondary paraphrase]**. Rasekhschaffe and Jones recommend combining different algorithms trained on different subsets rather than relying on one tuned model — [CFA Institute](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/research/financial-analysts-journal/2019/ip-machine-learning-for-stock-selection).

**What firms report about their own practice**
- Man AHL: before going live, it ran its first ML-based model on proprietary money for a year. It also uses an ML execution router that chooses among internal algorithms, dealer algorithms and its trading desk — [Risk.net, Quant hedge fund of the year 2018](https://www.risk.net/awards/5368836/quant-hedge-fund-of-the-year-man-ahl). Chief scientist Anthony Ledford spent about three years building an ML model. He called ML more robust than its predecessors but "hardly revolutionary" and warned "you always run the risk of overfitting" — [Bloomberg 2016](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2016-11-10/hedge-funds-beware-most-machine-learning-talk-is-really-hokum); [HKU seminar 2018](https://saasresearch.hku.hk/share/seminar/2018/LEDFORD_Anthony-20180521.pdf) **[2016–2018 sources]**.
- Man Group AlphaGPT (Man Insights, 13 Nov 2025; Ziang Fang and Jason Moore) is an agentic LLM workflow with an Idea Person, an Implementer (writes Python on Man's data and tools) and an Evaluator (significance tests, risk checks, economic reasoning), plus an orchestrator with hallucination safeguards. AI research faces **the same thresholds as human research and must have an economic rationale**. Every decision is logged, and the Investment Committee reviews hypotheses and outcomes. Man admits the system cannot be left unsupervised, that speed raises **multiple-testing risk** and "lucky" backtests, and that drift (implementing something other than the idea) remains a risk. It produces "dozens of viable concepts within minutes rather than days". Man expects LLM advantages to commoditize, with the durable edge in proprietary data, infrastructure and research philosophy — [Man Group](https://www.man.com/insights/what-ai-can-do-for-alpha) **[vendor]**. Bloomberg, via Hedgeweek, reports that AlphaGPT has produced "several dozen" signals approved for live trading at Man Numeric. The pipeline is hypothesis-first: "flipping a signal after seeing results isn't allowed for humans or AI" — [Hedgeweek](https://hedgeweek.com/news/man-group-deploys-agentic-ai-for-quant-signal-discovery). Man also announced a partnership integrating Anthropic's Claude with its platform — [BlueRating](https://www.bluerating.com/?p=862128).
- Bridgewater AIA Labs launched a fund of about $2bn in July 2024. It combines proprietary ML with LLMs from OpenAI, Anthropic and Perplexity and targets **macro** (growth, inflation, policy) rather than stock picking — [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2024/07/01/bridgewater-2-billion-fund-machine-learning-decision-making-openai-anthropic-perplexity). A 2025 return of 11.9% circulates only in wiki and blog sources — [LongTermWiki](https://www.longtermwiki.com/knowledge-base/organizations/labs/bridgewater-aia-labs) **[unverified; gross or net unknown]**.
- Two Sigma (Botte and Bao, Oct 2021) fits an unsupervised Gaussian Mixture Model to the factors in its Factor Lens to define market regimes, for macro-aware allocation and tail-risk management, not for stock selection — [Two Sigma](https://www.twosigma.com/articles/a-machine-learning-approach-to-regime-modeling).
- J.P. Morgan's "Big Data and AI Strategies" (Kolanovic and Krishnamachari, May 2017, about 280 pages) is a taxonomy of alternative data (human, process and machine generated) and of ML methods (supervised, unsupervised, deep, RL). Summaries say it judges machines best suited to short- and medium-term trading decisions — [PDF copy](https://www.smallake.kr/wp-content/uploads/2019/10/BIG-Data_AI-JPMmay2017.pdf); [smallake summary](https://smallake.kr/?p=24599) **[broker research, 2017]**.

**LLMs for return prediction**
- Lopez-Lira and Tang ("Can ChatGPT Forecast Stock Price Movements?"; latest revision Oct 2025, sample Oct 2021–May 2024): GPT-4 headline scores reach about 90% hit rates on the **non-tradable** initial reaction. Predictability is concentrated in small stocks and negative news, and only low-cost traders such as market makers can exploit it. **Strategy returns decline as LLM adoption rises**. Earlier drafts' "400%/650% cumulative" figures were pre-cost — [arXiv v5](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.07619v5).
- Glasserman and Lin (JFDS 2024) find GPT-3.5 sentiment backtests contaminated by **look-ahead bias** and a "distraction effect". Anonymizing company names raised in-sample returns, but the advantage largely vanished out-of-sample, so most of the in-sample effect came from knowledge of the future. Anonymization is recommended for de-biased backtests — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.17322).
- LLM return forecasts over-extrapolate recent performance much as humans do, even though returns show short-term reversal — [arXiv 2409.11540](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/2409.11540).

### Inferences
- The GBDT model in the T0 set matches what institutions run, but institutions (a) pool training across the universe, (b) ensemble several model types and training windows, (c) predict excess return over the market rather than the raw label, and (d) check that profits survive after dropping the smallest and least liquid names. Taiwan has many retail-dominated small caps, so the Avramov and Leippold results imply that a GBDT's edge may sit in exactly the stocks that cost the most to trade. A size and liquidity floor test is the institutional check.
- Hanauer's non-standard-error result means the spread across reasonable GBDT design choices (features, label horizon, retrain frequency) should be reported as a range, not as the single best variant.
- Institutions use LLMs as research agents with logging, pre-registered hypotheses and the same statistical bar as humans. That matches this project's "AI as researcher, not trader" stance. Any LLM-derived signal backtested on pre-cutoff news is suspect for look-ahead bias, by Glasserman's result.

### Gaps
- I found no primary Two Sigma publication on LLMs in equity selection, and no Goldman Sachs or BofA ML stock-selection report with performance data.
- The CFA Institute Research Foundation AI monographs were not retrieved.
- Man Numeric's specific use of gradient boosting is not public in what I found. AlphaGPT gives no live performance figures.

## 4. Emerging markets and Asia: factor efficacy and the TSMC concentration problem

### Takeaway
EM and Asian evidence supports low volatility, value, 52-week high, residual momentum and reversal, and ML composites built from standard features. Plain momentum and size are weaker. Retail-heavy markets (Taiwan, China) show liquidity- and short-horizon-driven predictability that is costly to harvest, and Chinese quants' 2024 "quant quake" showed the crowding risk of hidden small-cap tilts. TSMC is about 40–45% of TAIEX and about 58–60% of 0050. Benchmark providers and regulators handle this with caps that are being **relaxed**, so any diversified 20-stock rule runs a large structural active bet against TSMC.

### Cited Findings
**TSMC concentration and how benchmarks and managers handle it**

Coverage of the April 2026 rule change put it above 44% of TAIEX and about 13% of MSCI EM — [iTiger](https://www.itiger.com/news/1155803743); [Taipei Times, 2026-04-29](https://www.taipeitimes.com/News/biz/archives/2026/04/29/2003856408). A later MSCI factsheet, as reported, put TSMC at about 14.2% of MSCI EM, above India's 11.94% country weight — [Outlook Money](https://www.outlookmoney.com/invest/taiwanese-chipmaker-widens-weight-gap-with-india-in-msci-emerging-market-index). MSCI's August 2026 review raised Taiwan's EM weight from 26.6% to 26.85% — [Focus Taiwan](https://focustaiwan.tw/business/202608140007).
- TSMC's weight in **0050** was about 60.39% in early May 2026, 59.37% on 22 May (MediaTek 6.12%, Delta 4.38%) and about 58% in June 2026. The old 10% cap had been binding and had kept funds structurally underweight — [Taipei Times](https://www.taipeitimes.com/News/biz/archives/2026/04/29/2003856408); [Sherwood](https://sherwood.news/markets/tsm-surges-as-taiwan-eases-single-stock-investment-limits-for-funds/). **Factor efficacy in EM, Asia and China**
- Robeco's EM ML study (32 countries; top features price-to-52-week-high, idiosyncratic volatility, turnover) is in §3 — [Robeco](https://www.robeco.com/en-us/insights/2023/10/using-machine-learning-for-emerging-market-equity-returns).
- Low volatility in China (Blitz, Hanauer, van Vliet; *Journal of Asset Management* 2021): low-risk A-shares significantly beat high-risk ones. Volatility, not beta, is the driver. The VOL factor is not explained by Fama-French-Carhart, investment or profitability and has the strongest standalone performance. The likely source is behavioural bias among local retail investors — [Robeco](https://www.robeco.com/en-int/insights/2021/05/the-low-volatility-effect-in-china) **[vendor]**.
- China A-shares (Jansen, Swinkels, Zhou; PBFJ 2021; 32 anomalies over 2000–2019): the value, risk and trading families work. Size, quality and past-return families are weak, except residual momentum and reversal. Results hold across large, mid and small caps — [EUR](https://pure.eur.nl/en/publications/anomalies-in-the-china-a-share-market/). The counterpoint is Li, Liu, Liu and Wei (Management Science 2024). With mainboard breakpoints and value-weighting, **83.37% of 469 anomalies** show no significant quintile spread. Equal-weighted, all-breakpoint tests overweight microcaps with "very limited investment capacity" — [PolyU](https://research.polyu.edu.hk/en/publications/replicating-and-digesting-anomalies-in-the-chinese-a-share-market/).
- Long-only legs across regions (Blitz, Baltussen, van Vliet; FAJ 2020; 1963–2018; North America, Europe, **Japan, Asia ex-Japan**): long legs of value, momentum, profitability, investment and low-volatility deliver most of the premium and diversify better. Short legs are largely subsumed — [Robeco](https://www.robeco.com/en-int/insights/2021/02/when-equity-factors-drop-their-shorts) **[vendor]**.
- Taiwan-specific (mostly theses): a 2016 study finds a strong market premium but weak HML and SMB in TWSE industry portfolios — [AEPA 2016](https://www.doi.org/10.31203/AEPA.2016.13.4.003). An NCCU thesis finds Taiwan's size and value premia come mainly from small caps, while investment and profitability explain returns mainly among large caps. Most Taiwan characteristics' predictive power was stable or **improved** after publication — [NCCU thesis](https://thesis.lib.nccu.edu.tw/thesis/detail/dacc28faa4b1612e51d93fb97a2fc424) **[thesis]**. For the 52-week-high evidence in Taiwan, see §2 — [IJBESAR](https://ijbesar.ihu.gr/docs/volume16_issue1/16_01_07.pdf).
- Who wins and loses in Taiwan: Barber, Lee, Liu and Odean (RFS 2009, complete TWSE trade records for 1995–1999) find individuals' aggregate portfolio suffers a **3.8 percentage-point annual penalty**, nearly all from aggressive orders. Institutions gain 1.5 pp, with foreign institutions taking about half of institutional profits — [Berkeley PDF](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers%20current%20versions/justhowmuchdoindividualinvestorslose_rfs_2009.pdf). - MSCI publishes Taiwan factor indexes: MSCI Taiwan Momentum had 25 constituents and MSCI Taiwan IMI Minimum Volatility 65 as of 30 June 2026 — [MSCI Taiwan Momentum](https://www.msci.com/indexes/index/703828/msci-taiwan-momentum-index); [MSCI Taiwan Min Vol](https://www.msci.com/indexes/index/139442/msci-taiwan-minimum-volatility-index-twd).

### Inferences
- With TSMC at about 58–60% of 0050, the same-cash-flow comparison against 0050 is close to "my 20 stocks vs. mostly TSMC". In TSMC-led years, any diversified rule will look poor, and in years when TSMC lags it will look good, whatever its selection skill. An institution would decompose relative performance into (i) the TSMC/size bet and (ii) selection within the rest. A practical diagnostic, not a change to the official benchmark, is to also report results against a 30%-capped Taiwan 50 or an equal-weight Taiwan 50.
- Rules may hold TSMC as a stock (100% individual stocks is allowed). Whether a rule can ever hold TSMC at more than 1/20 weight is a material design choice that institutions handle explicitly through active-weight limits. The 2026 regulatory move to 25% shows Taiwan's own active managers needed that freedom.
- China's 2024 episode is the clearest Asian warning for factor-scan-derived rules. If the T0 candidates share a hidden small-cap or retail-sentiment tilt (margin growth, intraday momentum and short-horizon technical signals are prime suspects), they can fail together. Crowding and size-exposure checks across the 17 candidates are warranted.
- Taiwan individuals lose mainly through aggressive orders. For a daily-signal retail account, order type and timing (limit orders, not chasing the open) matter as much as signal choice.

## 5. Risk and drawdown at institutions: volatility targeting, drawdown rules, diversification across sleeves, stop-losses and long-only constraints

### Takeaway
Man's evidence supports portfolio-level volatility targeting for equity-like assets: it raises Sharpe modestly and thins the left tail. Academic replications show that volatility-managing individual factor portfolios often fails in real time, except for momentum. Drawdown rules are presented as **allocation cuts** that lower risk but also lower expected return unless the risk is redeployed. Their main value is detecting a broken strategy. Institutions stress that factor returns are fat-tailed and diversification can vanish in stress. Long-only books capture most of the factor premia, because the long legs carry most of the value.

### Cited Findings
- Man Group ("The Impact of Volatility Targeting"; Harvey, Hoyle, Korgaonkar, Rattray, Sargaison, Van Hemert; JPM 2018; 60+ assets, daily data from 1926, 10% target) finds volatility targeting **improves Sharpe for equities and credit**, and for balanced and risk-parity portfolios, with negligible effect for bonds, FX and commodities. It **reduces extreme left-tail returns** across assets, because sell-offs come with rising volatility, so the overlay acts like a trend-following layer. For US equities, Sharpe goes from about 0.40 to about 0.48–0.51, and the result is not sensitive to the volatility estimator — [Man Group](https://www.man.com/insights/the-impact-of-volatility-targeting); [Duke PDF](https://people.duke.edu/~charvey/Research/Published_Papers/P135_The_impact_of.pdf) **[authors affiliated with Man, which sells vol-targeted products]**.
- Counter-evidence for factor-level vol management: Cederburg, O'Doherty, Wang and Yan (JFE 2020, 103 equity strategies) find Moreira–Muir volatility-managed portfolios have positive spanning alphas, but realistic real-time versions **generally underperform** the unmanaged originals because the estimated weights are unstable. CXO counts 72 of 103 strategies worse out-of-sample. The exceptions with less degradation were momentum, ROE and BAB — [Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/does-portfolio-timing-based-on-volatility-signals-outperform-buy-and-hold/); [CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/volatility-effects/does-volatility-management-work-for-equity-factor-portfolios/). Momentum-specific vol scaling, by contrast, nearly doubles Sharpe (Barroso and Santa-Clara; Daniel and Moskowitz; see §2).
- Drawdown rules (Van Hemert, Ganz, Harvey, Rattray, Martin, Yawitch; "Drawdowns", JPM 2020, Man-affiliated authors): drawdown-based rules can help identify managers who have **lost their ability to outperform**. They reduce risk but change expected return. The example rule halves allocation when maximum drawdown breaches a threshold and restores it after half the drawdown is recovered. Without redeploying the freed risk, expected return generally falls. A lower Sharpe and positive autocorrelation make deep drawdowns more likely — [CXO Advisory](https://cxoadvisory.com/?p=33962); [Duke Scholars](https://scholars.duke.edu/publication/1461725). The same authors' book (*Strategic Risk Management*, Wiley 2021) frames risk management as part of alpha: defensive strategies, drawdown controls, volatility targeting and timed rebalancing — [Man Group](https://www.man.com/strategic-risk-management-book) **[vendor]**.
- Drawdown statistics (Rej, Seager, Bouchaud, CFM-affiliated): for a random walk with drift, typical drawdown depth scales as 1/Sharpe and drawdown **length** as 1/Sharpe². Long drawdowns are therefore normal for modest-Sharpe strategies — [arXiv 1707.01457](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1707.01457).
- Fat tails and illusory diversification (Arnott, Harvey, Kalesnik, Linnainmaa; JPM 2019): factor returns are negatively skewed and fat-tailed, and the drawdowns of the last 15 years "would never have been expected" under normality. Multi-factor diversification can vanish when factor correlations jump in certain conditions. Many investors use naive risk tools — [Research Affiliates](https://researchaffiliates.com/publications/articles/710-alices-adventures-in-factorland-three-blunders-that-plague-factor-investing).
- Crowding as a drawdown predictor: MSCI's crowding score ≥ 1 is associated with more frequent large factor drawdowns over the next 12 months (see §1) — [MSCI](https://www.msci.com/research-and-insights/blog-post/where-were-the-factor-crowds-this-summer) **[vendor]**.
- Diversifying sleeves: WorldQuant's 101 alphas average 15.9% pairwise correlation (§1). Gupta–Kelly's cross-factor average of factor momentum reaches a Sharpe of 0.84, above any single factor (§1). Both show that combining low-correlation streams raises Sharpe more than refining one stream — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1601.00991); [CXO](https://cxoadvisory.com/momentum-investing/ubiquitous-equity-factor-momentum).
- Long-only constraint: Blitz, Baltussen and van Vliet (FAJ 2020) find most of the factor value-add sits in the **long legs**, which have higher Sharpe and better diversification. Short legs carry "essentially the same exposure… with lower rewards and more risk", before shorting costs — [Robeco](https://www.robeco.com/en-int/insights/2021/02/when-equity-factors-drop-their-shorts); [CFA Institute](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/research/financial-analysts-journal/2020/when-equity-factors-drop-their-shorts). AQR, by contrast, finds momentum's long and short sides about equally profitable — [AQR](https://www.Aqr.Com/-/media/AQR/Documents/Journal-Articles/JPM-Fact-Fiction-and-Momentum-Investing.pdf?sc_lang=en). Avramov et al. find ML signals more profitable on the long side — [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=3450322).
- Turnover as a risk to returns: daily-rebalanced momentum is net-negative while weekly or slower is positive (AQR Craftsmanship Alpha) — [Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/portfolio-craftsmanship-just-important-choosing-investment-style/). For short-term signals, a buy-10%/hold-50% buffer turns net alpha from negative to above 6% (Robeco) — [Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/alpha-from-short-term-signals/).

### Inferences
- For a long-only 20-stock account with no leverage, "volatility targeting" can only mean holding cash when realized volatility is high, since the account cannot lever up when volatility is low. Man's evidence (equity Sharpe 0.40 → about 0.5, thinner left tail) supports a simple cash-scaling overlay on the **whole** account. Cederburg's results warn against fitting separate scaling parameters per signal. The cleanest institutional case for dynamic scaling is a momentum-heavy book (Barroso, Daniel–Moskowitz).
- Drawdown rules should be used mainly as **diagnostics** that a T0 rule is broken (Van Hemert), calibrated to the rule's expected Sharpe. A rule with a live Sharpe of 0.5 should be expected to have multi-year drawdowns (length ∝ 1/SR²), so a tight stop would retire good rules at random.
- Turnover estimate (my arithmetic, using Taiwan's standard costs, which are not sourced in these notes: 0.1425% brokerage each side, often discounted, plus 0.3% securities transaction tax on sells). Thirty orders a month in a 20-stock equal-weight book trades about 150% of the account a month (buys plus sells), which is about 75% one-way, or roughly 900% a year. That is about 9 round trips a year × about 0.585% ≈ **5% a year in explicit costs** before slippage. This is the same regime where Robeco's short-term signals lost more than two-thirds of gross alpha. Institutions' answer is rank buffers (buy top N, hold until rank falls below 2–5 × N) and weekly or slower rebalancing, not fewer signals.
- Long-only is not a major handicap for these factor families according to Robeco. The main long-only problem in Taiwan is the benchmark's TSMC concentration (§4), not the lack of shorts.

### Gaps
- I found no institution that publishes a concrete **strategy-level stop-loss** threshold. Kaminski and Lo's "When do stop-loss rules stop losses?" was not retrieved.
- I found no institutional study of drawdown control specific to long-only small accounts with monthly cash inflows.

## 6. Strategy evaluation and retirement: incubation, live tracking, haircuts and when to stop

### Takeaway
Institutions expect backtests to overstate live results heavily. Median Sharpe haircuts are about 50–75%, larger for complex strategies and for anything that relied on microcaps. They require higher statistical bars (t > 3, nonlinear multiple-testing haircuts) and incubate new models on house money before client money. Rules are retired for **structural** reasons (broken rationale, crowding, decayed or uncapturable premium, implementation drift), not because of a bad run that lies within expected tracking error. Even good factors have had 5-, 10- and sometimes 20-year underperformance periods.

### Cited Findings
- Backtest vs live: Suhonen, Lennkh and Perez (JPM 2017) studied 215 bank-sponsored alternative-beta strategies from about 15–17 banks. The median Sharpe was **1.20 in backtest vs 0.31 live, a 73% haircut**. Only 18 of 215 (8.4%) matched or beat their backtest Sharpe live, and 30% had a negative live Sharpe. The most complex strategies' haircuts exceeded the simplest strategies' by more than 30 percentage points. The commentary concludes the industry's 50% haircut "is not conservative enough" — [Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/?p=36892); [CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/big-ideas/live-performance-of-alternative-beta-products/); [MathInvestor](https://mathinvestor.org/2017/08/backtest-overfitting-in-smart-beta-investments/).
- Harvey and Liu ("Backtesting", JPM 2015): convert Sharpe to a t-stat, adjust the p-value for the number of tests, and convert back. The haircut is **nonlinear**: top Sharpes are lightly penalized and marginal ones heavily, and marginal strategies "should be thrown out". A flat 50% haircut is a "serious mistake" — [Duke PDF](https://people.duke.edu/~charvey/Research/Published_Papers/P120_Backtesting.PDF); [Man Institute summary](https://www.man.com/insights/backtesting). Harvey, Liu and Zhu (RFS 2016, 316 factors) recommend t ≥ 3.0 for new factors — [CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/big-ideas/taming-the-factor-zoo/). A Federal Reserve paper argues that attributing the whole factor zoo to p-hacking is wrong — [Federal Reserve](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/feds/files/2019016pap.pdf).
- Arnott, Harvey, Kalesnik and Linnainmaa (JPM 2019): excluding the bottom 2% of stocks by market cap leaves **64% of factors** without significant alpha. Backtests also flatter factors that start cheap and end expensive, and misattribute returns that come from other factor loadings. The three questions to ask: does the factor make sense, how crowded is it, and what are realistic trading costs? — [Queen's copy of the paper](https://www.econ.queensu.ca/sites/econ.queensu.ca/files/Econ%20870/Arnott%2C%20et%20al%20Alices%20Adventures%20in%20Factorland%2C%202019.pdf); [Research Affiliates](https://researchaffiliates.com/publications/articles/710-alices-adventures-in-factorland-three-blunders-that-plague-factor-investing) **[64% figure from a hosted copy, not verified against the journal]**.
- Post-publication decay is 26% out-of-sample and 58% after publication (McLean–Pontiff) — [PDF](https://Www.Gwern.net/doc/economics/2016-mclean.pdf). LLM-signal returns are decaying with adoption (Lopez-Lira–Tang) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.07619v5).
- Incubation: Man AHL ran its first ML model on **proprietary capital for a year** before going live — [Risk.net](https://www.risk.net/awards/5368836/quant-hedge-fund-of-the-year-man-ahl). Man's AlphaGPT signals pass the same investment-committee process as human research. Hypotheses are fixed in advance, and flipping a signal after seeing results is banned — [Man Group](https://www.man.com/insights/what-ai-can-do-for-alpha); [Hedgeweek](https://hedgeweek.com/news/man-group-deploys-agentic-ai-for-quant-signal-discovery).
- How long to judge: Swedroe (republished by Advisor Perspectives) finds every major factor had significant 5-year and 10-year losing periods, and some had 20-year periods, except momentum at 20 years — [Advisor Perspectives](https://www.advisorperspectives.com/articles/2018/11/05/what-to-do-when-an-investment-strategy-performs-poorly). Robeco warns that factor tracking error makes multi-year relative underperformance "inevitable", and that judging managers on 3–5-year windows makes factors look riskier than they are — [Robeco](https://www.robeco.com/en-uk/insights/2017/05/factor-investing-challenges-underperforming-the-benchmark) **[vendor]**.
- When to stop (AQR): Asness advises surviving drawdowns rather than timing them, sizing bets so you can stick with them, and abandoning a factor only if the reason it works ("why you think that factor works") is gone, for example by being arbitraged away. He judged that "we're not even close" to that for value — [ai-CIO](https://www.ai-cio.com/news/asness-factors-could-be-arbitraged-away-by-etfs/); [Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/cliff-asnesss-aqr-view-on-factor-timing/).
- Drawdown-based retirement: drawdown rules can flag managers who have lost skill, at a cost to expected return if risk is not redeployed (Van Hemert et al. 2020) — [CXO Advisory](https://cxoadvisory.com/?p=33962). Drawdown depth and length scale with 1/SR and 1/SR² — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1707.01457).
- Robustness to design choices: across more than 1,000 ML specifications, the non-standard error is 59% larger than the standard error (Hanauer) — [Poznań abstract](https://ue.poznan.pl/wydarzenia/17th-online-seminar-in-finance-matthias-hanauer-robeco-technical-university-of-munich). Profits that vanish without microcaps or distressed stocks are a red flag (Avramov et al.) — [CUHK](https://cbk.bschool.cuhk.edu.hk/the-limitations-of-using-ai-to-pick-stocks/).

### Inferences
- The 17 T0 candidates were selected from about 39,676 tested combinations. Under Harvey–Liu-style adjustment, the marginal ones should be expected to lose most of their edge, and Suhonen's median of about 73% is a reasonable prior for complex combinations. Survival should be judged on (1) the haircut-adjusted Sharpe, (2) robustness when the smallest and least liquid Taiwan names are excluded, (3) net-of-cost results with realistic turnover, and (4) a clear economic story. Rules that fail (2) or (4) are the institutional "throw out" cases.
- Incubation analogue: run surviving rules as paper or forward-tracked portfolios for a fixed pre-committed period, with Man AHL's one year as the reference point. Do not retune during incubation, which is the "no flipping after seeing results" rule. Define retirement triggers in advance: structural break, crowding or style concentration, costs above budget, or drawdown beyond what the rule's expected Sharpe makes plausible.
- With about 11 years of Taiwan data and a 20-stock book, short live windows cannot separate skill from luck at plausible Sharpe levels (Swedroe, Robeco). Retirement decisions should rest on structural evidence and pre-set statistical thresholds, not on a 6–12-month comparison with 0050, whose TSMC concentration (§4) dominates short-run relative results.

### Gaps
- I found no institution that publishes explicit numeric incubation lengths or retirement thresholds beyond Man AHL's one-year prop-money test. Two Sigma, Renaissance and AQR internal signal-retirement criteria are not public in what I found.
- AQR's "Factor Timing is Hard" (2017 Perspectives) and "The August of our Discontent" pieces were found but not read in full.
