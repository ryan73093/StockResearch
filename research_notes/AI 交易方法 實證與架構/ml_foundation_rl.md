# Non-LLM machine learning for stock returns: cross-sectional deep learning, time-series foundation models and RL portfolios (out-of-sample evidence up to Oct 2026)

Notes compiled 2026-10-09. Labels used: **IS** = in-sample / backtest chosen by the authors; **OOS** = out-of-sample test period held out from model fitting (still a backtest); **LIVE** = real money / published fund record. Unless stated otherwise, all academic numbers are gross of trading costs.

## 1. Cross-sectional ML (Gu–Kelly–Xiu 2020, replications incl. Asia, transformers / AIPM, the "virtue of complexity" debate, costs, long-only)

### Takeaway
Out of sample, nonlinear models (trees, shallow neural nets) beat linear/OLS return predictors on monthly stock returns, but the gain is small in R² terms (≈0.3–0.4% per month in the US) and is turned into large gross long-short Sharpe ratios mainly through high turnover and hard-to-arbitrage (small, illiquid, distressed) stocks. Most of the edge shrinks or disappears once microcaps are excluded and realistic costs applied, unless costs are built into the learning objective or slower horizons are used. Transformer "AI pricing models" report the highest gross Sharpe ratios yet, but these are gross, long-short, and the authors themselves note much of the performance comes from illiquid stocks.

### Cited Findings

**Gu, Kelly & Xiu (2020), Review of Financial Studies 33(5) — US, OOS 1987–2016**
- Sample: individual US stocks March 1957–Dec 2016; 18 years training (1957–74), 12 years validation (1975–86), 30 years OOS testing (1987–2016); 920 features in the full-OLS model. — [GKX, RFS 2020 (author PDF)](https://dachxiu.chicagobooth.edu/download/ML.pdf)
- Monthly stock-level OOS R²: OLS with all 920 features **−3.46%** (worse than forecasting zero); GLM with group lasso 0.19%; random forest 0.33%; boosted trees 0.34%; NN1 0.33%, peaking at **0.40% for NN3**; NN4/NN5 do not improve on NN3 ("benefits of 'deep' learning are limited"). — [GKX 2020](https://dachxiu.chicagobooth.edu/download/ML.pdf)
- Among the top-1,000 stocks by market cap, trees and NNs reach OOS R² of 0.52%–0.70%; the authors read this as evidence ML "is not merely picking up small scale inefficiencies driven by illiquidity." — [GKX 2020](https://dachxiu.chicagobooth.edu/download/ML.pdf)
- Economic value (OOS 1987–2016, gross): value-weighted long-short decile spread on NN forecasts Sharpe **1.35** (NN4: 2.26%/month mean, 5.80% monthly s.d.); equal-weighted NN4 Sharpe **2.45**; the benchmark OLS long-short gets 0.61 (VW) and 0.83 (EW). Market timing of the S&P 500 with NN forecasts: Sharpe 0.77 vs 0.51 buy-and-hold. — [GKX 2020](https://dachxiu.chicagobooth.edu/download/ML.pdf)
- VW long-short Sharpe by NN depth: NN3 1.20, NN4 1.35, NN5 1.15. Max drawdown of VW NN4 long-short 51.78% (NN3 30.84%). — [GKX 2020, Tables 7–8](https://dachxiu.chicagobooth.edu/download/ML.pdf)
- Turnover: NN1–NN5 long-short portfolios turn over **110%–130% per month**; trees and penalized regressions slightly higher; reference points short-term reversal 172.6%/month and size 22.9%/month. The paper does not net out transaction costs. — [GKX 2020](https://dachxiu.chicagobooth.edu/download/ML.pdf)
- Dominant predictors across all methods: price trends (momentum, industry momentum, short-term reversal), then liquidity (market value, dollar volume, bid-ask spread), then volatility/beta. — [GKX 2020](https://dachxiu.chicagobooth.edu/download/ML.pdf)

**Where the gains come from / costs — Avramov, Cheng & Metzker (2023), Management Science 69(5)**
- Deep-learning signals extract profitability from difficult-to-arbitrage stocks and high limits-to-arbitrage market states; excluding microcaps, distressed stocks, or high-volatility episodes "considerably attenuates profitability"; performance deteriorates further with reasonable trading costs due to high turnover and extreme tangency weights. They also find the signals are profitable on the long side and in recent years, with low downside risk. — [Avramov, Cheng & Metzker, Management Science 2023 (SSRN)](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=3450322)
- Press coverage of the paper: excluding microcaps cuts adjusted returns by 62%, excluding non-rated firms by 68%, excluding distressed firms by 80%; Cheng: an average investor "would struggle to achieve meaningful alpha after taking transaction costs into account." (Magnitudes from press, not re-checked against tables.) — [finews.asia](https://www.finews.asia/finance/32603-cuhk-ai-based-stock-picking-overrated)
- Note the tension with GKX: GKX report higher *R²* among large stocks, while Avramov et al. find the *portfolio profits* concentrate in hard-to-trade stocks. — compare [GKX 2020](https://dachxiu.chicagobooth.edu/download/ML.pdf) and [Avramov et al. 2023](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=3450322)

**Cost-aware ML — Jensen, Kelly, Malamud & Pedersen, "Machine Learning and the Implementable Efficient Frontier" (RFS, in press; WP Aug 2022)**
- ML return forecasts that ignore trading costs "rely excessively on fleeting small-scale characteristics, resulting in poor net returns"; learning portfolio weights directly with a cost-aware economic objective gives a strong net-of-cost frontier. — [SSRN 4187217](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=4187217)
- Slides: OOS test 1981–2020, net of costs for a $10bn portfolio by 2020; standard ML implementations yield a "poor frontier"; quality and value signals matter most for the implementable frontier, short-term reversal has limited impact for a large investor. — [Pedersen slides, CBS](https://www.cbs.dk/files/cbs.dk/slides_lasse_heje_pedersen.pdf)

**Horizon / turnover — Blitz, Hanauer, Hoogteijling & Howard (Robeco), "The Term Structure of Machine Learning Alpha" (2023)**
- One-month-horizon ML strategies show impressive gross alphas but were mainly profitable before 2004; net of costs they deteriorated significantly after 2004 (US). Models trained for 3-, 6-, 12-month horizons had clearly better net performance over 2004–2021, trade less, and tilt toward quality/value; efficient (buy-hold-style) portfolio construction rules helped at every horizon. (Source is the authors' firm's summary.) — [Robeco insight, Jul 2023](https://www.robeco.com/en-uk/insights/2023/07/the-term-structure-of-machine-learning-alpha)

**International / Asian replications**
- China — Leippold, Wang & Zhou (2022), JFE 145:64–82: liquidity (not momentum) is the most important predictor; dominant retail presence boosts short-term predictability, especially for small stocks; large stocks and SOEs are predictable at longer horizons; OOS performance "remains economically significant after transaction costs." — [SFI abstract](https://www.sfi.ch/en/publications/machine-learning-in-the-chinese-stock-market)
- Global — Cakici, Fieberg, Metko & Zaremba (2023), J. Economic Dynamics & Control 155:104725: 46 markets, 148 characteristics; most predictive power comes from simple factor types (momentum, reversal, value, size); forecast combination works best; performance depends heavily on firm size and recency of information, and varies across countries with the number of listed firms and idiosyncratic risk (limits to arbitrage). — [ICM open repository](https://open.icm.edu.pl/items/f88ffcae-dc39-44e1-b48a-602d7612de55)
- Country-level "Global Edition" (Cakici & Zaremba WP): long-short 1.69%/month, Sharpe 1.57; predictability driven by mispricing, boosted by limits to arbitrage, and "gradually diminishes over time as global markets mature." — [ICM working paper PDF](https://open.icm.edu.pl/bitstreams/7d3aefed-ecb3-4bc2-b2fc-850e0c4d622e/download)
- Emerging markets — Hanauer & Kalsbach (2023), Emerging Markets Review (DOI 10.1016/j.ememar.2023.101022): nonlinear models beat linear OOS; hard-to-arbitrage stocks easier to predict but that advantage shrinks for nonlinear models; profits survive trading costs, short-sale constraints and a large-cap-only universe. — [TUM news](https://www.fa.mgt.tum.de/fm/news/article/matthias-hanauer-and-tobias-kalsbach-awarded-runner-up-of-the-cfa-germany-investment-research-award-2024/)
- Same study per Robeco: >15,000 stocks, 32 EM countries, 1990–2021; long-short ≈0.8%/month for linear models, ≈1.0% for RF/GBRT, ≈1.2% for NN and ensemble. — [Robeco, Oct 2023](https://www.robeco.com/en-us/insights/2023/10/using-machine-learning-for-emerging-market-equity-returns)
- Taiwan replication: see Section 4 (Bui, Kong, Lin & Lin 2023).

**Transformers in the cross-section — Kelly, Kuznetsov, Malamud & Xu, "Artificial Intelligence Asset Pricing Models" (NBER WP 33351, Jan 2025, revised June 2026; not peer-reviewed)**
- Data: JKP US characteristics 1963–2022, 132 characteristics, nano caps (<1st NYSE percentile) excluded; expanding-window OOS test Feb 1968–Dec 2022. — [NBER w33351 PDF](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w33351/w33351.pdf)
- OOS annualized SDF Sharpe ratios 1968–2022 (gross): FF6 1.01, HXZ 1.77, SY 1.37, DHS 1.21; linear characteristic SDF (BSV) 3.60; DKKM nonlinear random-feature SDF 3.87; linear attention 3.89; MLP 4.31; **nonlinear transformer 4.57**. Post-2002 subsample: FF6 0.74, BSV 2.03, DKKM 2.41, MLP 3.07, transformer 3.37 (every model loses Sharpe after 2002: transformer −26%, MLP −29%, DKKM −38%, BSV −44%, DHS −62%). — [NBER w33351, Table 1](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w33351/w33351.pdf)
- Complexity: Sharpe rises from 3.8 for a 1-block transformer (~100k parameters) to 4.6 for 10 blocks (~1M parameters), slope "not yet flattened." — [NBER w33351](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w33351/w33351.pdf)
- Size: the authors state "it is likely that much of the performance of these models is generated from the relatively illiquid subset of stocks"; within-size-group retraining shows Sharpe rising monotonically from mega to micro caps for all models; the transformer's *relative* gain is largest among mega/large caps. The text reports no transaction-cost or turnover analysis (search of the full text for "cost"/"turnover" finds only computational cost and turnover-based linkage groups). — [NBER w33351, §4.5.3](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w33351/w33351.pdf)
- A newsletter reports a mega-cap Sharpe of 1.84 vs 1.18 for competitors; not verified in the paper text (the numbers are only in a figure). — [AI Street newsletter](https://aistreet.beehiiv.com/p/bridgewater-ceo-ai-fund-generating-unique-alpha)

**"Virtue of complexity" debate**
- Kelly, Malamud & Zhou (2024), Journal of Finance 79(1):459–503: claim that "complex" models with more parameters than observations (random Fourier features) improve market-timing return prediction relative to simple models. — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfinan/v79y2024i1p459-503.html); [NBER w30217](https://nber.org/papers/w30217)
- Nagel (2025), "Seemingly Virtuous Complexity in Return Prediction" (NBER w34104 / BFI WP 2025-104): with training windows as short as 12 months and thousands of RFF features, the forecast collapses to a similarity-weighted average of the 12 training returns — effectively a volatility-timed momentum strategy that happened to work historically; on artificial data with reversals the method builds the same strategy and performs poorly. — [BFI WP 2025-104](https://bfi.uchicago.edu/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/BFI_WP_2025-104.pdf); [IDEAS w34104](https://ideas.repec.org/p/nbr/nberwo/34104.html)

### Inferences
- The robust, replicated result is "nonlinear > linear OOS, gross". The size of the *net, long-only, large-cap* gain is much smaller and less well documented; for a single retail investor in a mid-size market, the relevant evidence is Avramov et al., Jensen et al. and Blitz et al., not the headline long-short Sharpe ratios.
- Headline Sharpe ratios rose from ~1.35 (GKX 2020, VW decile spread) to ~4.6 (AIPM 2025, SDF portfolio), but the latter is an SDF/tangency-style portfolio on 132 characteristics including micro caps, gross of costs, and loses a large share after 2002 — it is not comparable to an implementable long-only portfolio.
- Shallow models generally match or beat deeper ones on monthly data (GKX NN3 > NN4/5; Taiwan NN2 > deeper, see §4), whereas the AIPM claims gains from depth; the Nagel critique shows "complexity" gains can be an artefact of what the model implicitly reduces to. Treat complexity claims as unsettled.
- Monthly turnover of 110–130% (GKX) is incompatible with a high-cost market. Taiwan's statutory 0.3% sell-side transaction tax plus brokerage (a general fact, not sourced in these notes) would consume a large share of a monthly-rebalanced long-short gross spread; longer horizons and cost-aware objectives are the documented mitigations.

### Gaps
- Exact after-cost Sharpe / breakeven costs from Jensen et al. (RFS) — only qualitative findings and slides retrieved.
- Whether the long-only legs alone beat the market after costs in GKX/AIPM — neither paper reports long-only net results in the parts retrieved.
- Other critiques of "virtue of complexity" (e.g. Buncic; Cartea et al.) and any KMZ reply — not retrieved.
- Japanese and Korean cross-sectional ML replications — not retrieved in this pass (Cakici et al. cover 46 markets but country-level tables were not checked).

## 2. Time-series foundation models (Kronos, TimesFM, Chronos, Moirai, Lag-Llama, TimeGPT) on financial prices

### Takeaway
General-purpose TSFMs (TimesFM, Chronos, Moirai, TimeGPT) used zero-shot are essentially no better than a random walk for stock *returns* (skill ≈10⁻³, OOS R² negative); they are more useful for *volatility*, where they are competitive with but do not reliably beat HAR-family econometric baselines unless fine-tuned or ensembled. Finance-specific pretraining (Kronos; Rahimikia et al.'s finance-pretrained Chronos/TimesFM) closes much of the gap, but in the largest independent test still does not beat CatBoost on daily returns. Pretraining-corpus overlap with test periods is a real leakage risk for off-the-shelf checkpoints.

### Cited Findings

**Rahimikia, Ni & Wang, "Re(Visiting) Time Series Foundation Models in Finance" (arXiv 2511.18578, Nov 2025) — the largest independent test**
- Daily firm-level excess returns 1990–2023 across 94 countries; OOS 2001–2023 expanding window; US test sample 18.1M daily observations / 10,171 securities (price ≥ $5); 8 test markets incl. US, Hong Kong, **Taiwan**, South Korea, Germany, UK, India, Australia. — [arXiv 2511.18578 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.18578v1)
- Benchmarks: OLS OOS R² ≈ −0.47%, CatBoost ≈ −0.10%, directional accuracy just above 51%; CatBoost (252-day window) long-short 46.50% annualized, Sharpe 6.79 **before transaction costs** (daily rebalanced). — [arXiv 2511.18578](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.18578v1)
- Zero-shot: Chronos-large (512-day context) R² −1.37%, accuracy ~51%, long-short 20.17%/yr; TimesFM-500M R² **−2.80%**, accuracy just below 50%, long-short **−1.47%/yr**. Fine-tuning mostly *worsened* results (Chronos-large improved statistically but not economically). — [arXiv 2511.18578](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.18578v1)
- Pretrained from scratch on finance data yearly (no look-ahead; 396 Chronos and 264 TimesFM models): Chronos-small at 512 days 36.84%/yr, Sharpe 5.42; with global data, financial factors and synthetic augmentation Chronos-small reaches 51.74% accuracy vs CatBoost 51.16%, but 41.89%/yr Sharpe 6.78 vs CatBoost 47.25%/yr Sharpe 6.46. Authors: TSFMs "still fit less well than the best benchmarks" but decay more slowly; long leg outperforms short leg across all classes; non-US results broadly consistent. — [arXiv 2511.18578](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.18578v1)
- Leakage: authors note off-the-shelf models may carry look-ahead bias because pretraining corpora can overlap the test period; they pretrain their own models each year to avoid it. — [arXiv 2511.18578](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.18578v1)
- Abstract summary: "off-the-shelf pre-trained TSFMs perform poorly in zero-shot and fine-tuning settings." — [arXiv abs 2511.18578](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.18578)

**Noguer i Alonso & Pereira Franklin, "Pretrained Time-Series Foundation Models for Financial Return Forecasting" (arXiv 2606.27100, Jun 2026)**
- AAPL, AMZN, GOOG, JPM, META; 20-business-day horizon; train to 2024-08-21, evaluation 2024-08-22 to Feb 2026, 10 rolling origins; zero-shot TimeGPT, TimesFM-2.5, Moirai-2.0, Chronos, Chronos-2 vs per-ticker NBEATS, NHITS, PatchTST, iTransformer, KAN. — [arXiv 2606.27100](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.27100)
- TSFMs win 8 of 10 tasks on MAE, but skill scores vs a zero-return random walk are "small and sparse, on the order of 10⁻³"; the one-sided Diebold–Mariano test rejects equal-or-worse accuracy vs random walk only for Chronos on AMZN and Moirai-2.0 on GOOG; authors state results do not show reliable alpha or trading performance; MAE only, no trading metrics. — [arXiv 2606.27100](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.27100)

**Kronos (Shi et al., arXiv 2508.02739, Aug 2025; GitHub shiyu-coder/Kronos)**
- Decoder-only model over tokenized OHLCV "K-lines"; pretrained on >12 billion K-line records from 45 exchanges (appendix says "over 40 exchanges across more than 30 countries" — internal inconsistency); sizes small 24.7M, base 102.3M, large 499.2M parameters. Pretraining data extends to June 2024; all test periods begin July 2024 ("strict temporal separation"). Test exchanges include XTAI (Taiwan) as an **out-of-distribution** market. — [Kronos, arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.02739); [abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.02739)
- Headline claims (abstract): RankIC +93% vs leading TSFM and +87% vs best non-pretrained baseline on price forecasting; 9% lower volatility MAE; 22% better synthetic-data fidelity. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.02739)
- Absolute numbers behind the headlines (averages across assets/frequencies): price-series IC Kronos-L 0.0440 (RankIC 0.0267) vs zero-shot TimesFM 0.0052, Chronos-L 0.0060, Moirai ≈0, TimeMOE-B 0.0174, and trained baselines DLinear 0.0214, TimesNet 0.0317. Return forecasting RankIC Kronos-L 0.0675 vs best trained baselines DLinear 0.0423 / NSTransformer 0.0421, zero-shot Moirai-L 0.0527, TimeMOE-S 0.0477, TimesFM 0.0408, Chronos-L 0.0286. — [Kronos appendix tables 14–17](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.02739)
- Taiwan (XTAI): price IC Kronos-L 0.0448 vs DLinear 0.0394, NSTransformer 0.0334, PatchTST 0.0328, zero-shot Chronos-L 0.0080; return RankIC Kronos-L 0.0609 vs best zero-shot Moirai-S 0.0494. — [Kronos appendix](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.02739)
- Volatility (realized vol MAE, lower better): Kronos-L 0.0370 vs GARCH 0.0405, ARCH 0.0448; Taiwan Kronos-L 0.0213 vs GARCH 0.0240. — [Kronos Table 18](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.02739)
- Investment simulation: long-only top-k/drop-n on China A-shares (Qlib), CSI300 k=50/n=5 and CSI800 k=200/n=10, min 5-day holding, 0.15% cost per trade; Kronos-L annualized excess return 21.93% / IR 1.42 (CSI300), 19.74% / IR 1.88 (CSI800); best baseline Moment-L avg 16.81% / IR 1.37, PatchTST 14.55% / IR 1.25. Backtest period not stated in retrieved text; no leakage/limitations section found. — [Kronos Table 10](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.02739)

**Volatility vs returns — independent evidence**
- Brini (arXiv 2607.05291, Jul 2026): 9 zero-shot TSFMs vs 8 econometric models on 50 assets; no uniform gain; architecture choice matters more than "foundation vs econometric"; on 40 US equities, HAR is in the 10% Model Confidence Set at all horizons under QLIKE while Chronos-Bolt and TimesFM-2.5 are not (1-day QLIKE HAR 0.199 vs TimesFM-2.5 0.217, Chronos-Bolt-S 0.222; 22-day 0.582 vs 0.724/0.710); under MSE at 22 days TimesFM-2.5 (64.2) and Chronos-Bolt-S (64.6) beat plain HAR (72.4) but not Log-HAR (63.1) or ARFIMA (61.5); an equal-weight TTM + Log-HAR blend is in the MCS for 98–100% of assets. — [arXiv 2607.05291](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.05291)
- Goel et al. (arXiv 2505.11163, 2025): zero-shot TimesFM is a "reasonable baseline" for realized volatility, but incremental fine-tuning is essential; fine-tuned variants statistically outperform traditional models (authors' own evaluation). — [arXiv 2505.11163](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.11163v1)
- A survey of TSFMs in finance notes TimesFM's zero-shot strength is shown mainly on non-financial benchmarks (ETT, Weather, Electricity) and that VaR/ES use needs quantile heads. — [ACM survey](https://dl.acm.org/doi/full/10.1145/3785706.3785728)

### Inferences
- For *returns*, the evidence is consistent: off-the-shelf zero-shot TSFMs ≈ random walk (or worse), and their "wins" over trained deep baselines are wins among models that all have near-zero skill. Kronos's large *relative* improvements (+93% RankIC) are over baselines with IC ≈ 0.005; its absolute IC (~0.04–0.07) is in the range of ordinary single-factor signals, and a simple DLinear gets ~88% of Kronos-L's price IC in Taiwan.
- For *volatility*, TSFMs are competitive (Kronos beats GARCH by ~9–11% MAE; fine-tuned TimesFM beats some benchmarks), but HAR/Log-HAR remain hard to beat; volatility forecasting is the more promising use (risk scaling, position sizing), not stock picking.
- Leakage: for the Noguer i Alonso test window (Aug 2024–Feb 2026), the 2025-vintage checkpoints (TimesFM-2.5, Moirai-2.0, Chronos-2) were released during or after that window, so their corpora could overlap it (release timing is from general knowledge, not verified here). Any backtest of an off-the-shelf TSFM on dates before its data cutoff is potentially contaminated; Kronos's June-2024 cutoff means only post-July-2024 data is clean for Kronos.
- Kronos's investment simulation is the authors' own, on one market, with the backtest period unstated — treat as IS-quality evidence until independently replicated.

### Gaps
- No independent peer-reviewed replication of Kronos's results found; no live track record.
- No Lag-Llama or TimeGPT evaluation specific to stock returns found beyond TimeGPT's inclusion in arXiv 2606.27100.
- The official pretraining corpora of TimesFM / Chronos / Moirai were not checked for inclusion of stock price series — overlap is a stated risk, not a verified fact.
- Taiwan-specific per-market numbers from Rahimikia et al. not retrieved (only "broadly consistent with US").

## 3. Reinforcement learning for portfolios (FinRL, DRL papers, reproducibility, vs equal weight / buy-and-hold after costs)

### Takeaway
There is no robust out-of-sample evidence that deep RL beats 1/N or buy-and-hold after costs. The broadest test (SAC across seven datasets, >300 years of OOS data) finds no systematic outperformance of 1/N even without frictions and negative net returns at 0.1% costs. The FinRL ecosystem itself acknowledges reproducibility problems, backtest overfitting via repeated retuning, low signal-to-noise and survivorship bias. Positive results exist mostly as single-study backtests.

### Cited Findings
- Kruthof & Müller (2025), "Can deep reinforcement learning beat 1/N", Finance Research Letters 75 (DOI 10.1016/j.frl.2025.106866): Soft Actor-Critic on seven equity datasets, >300 years of OOS data in total; SAC "demonstrates market timing potential" but "does not systematically outperform a 1/N benchmark" frictionless; at 0.1% transaction costs its high turnover produces **negative net returns** while 1/N stays robust; no consistent tail-risk advantage. — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/finlet/v75y2025ics154461232500131x.html)
- Background: DeMiguel, Garlappi & Uppal (2009, RFS) showed 1/N is hard to beat out of sample; a 2026 paper summarizes that naive 1/N "consistently match[es] or exceed[s]" optimized portfolios. — [arXiv 2602.17098](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.17098)
- FinRL (Liu et al., 2020): library supporting DQN, DDPG, PPO, SAC, A2C, TD3 on NASDAQ-100, DJIA, S&P 500, HSI, SSE 50, CSI 300 with transaction-cost and risk-aversion settings; results are backtests. — [arXiv 2011.09607](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.09607)
- FinRL-Meta (NeurIPS 2022 Datasets & Benchmarks): DRL trading works are "difficult to reproduce"; near-real environments are hard to build because of low signal-to-noise, survivorship bias and backtest overfitting. — [FinRL-Meta paper](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2022/file/0bf54b80686d2c4dc0808c2e98d430f7-Paper-Datasets_and_Benchmarks.pdf)
- FinRL team (2023), "Dynamic Datasets and Market Environments for Financial RL": existing research mainly reports backtests; researchers may retune and retrain agents to improve backtests, causing overfitting (flagged as information leakage). — [arXiv 2304.13174](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.13174)
- FinRL-Podracer: benchmarking FinRL, RLlib and Stable-Baselines3 found it "difficult for them to effectively train a profitable trading agent," and there is no criterion to detect over/underfitting during training. — [FinRL-Podracer PDF](https://www.idea.edu.cn/guojian/assets/papers/FinRL-Podracer-2021.pdf)
- FinRL team also published a dedicated method to address DRL backtest overfitting (crypto, 2022). — [arXiv 2209.05559](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2209.05559)
- PRUDEX-Compass (2023): SAC is "the base model of many popular FinRL methods"; proposes systematic multi-dimension evaluation of RL in markets because single-metric backtests are insufficient. — [arXiv 2302.00586](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2302.00586)
- FinRL Contests (2025 paper): standardized tasks, curated datasets and withheld OOS evaluation introduced to improve reproducibility/comparability. — [Wiley AIE 2025](https://ietresearch.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1049/aie2.12004)
- Counter-evidence (single study): factor-based DRL (CAFPO) reports beating equal-weight, value-weight, Markowitz and vanilla DRL OOS with 24.6% compound return and Sharpe 0.94. — [PLOS ONE 2025](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0332779)
- A public replication (Chinese pharma/biotech, PPO vs EW / mean-variance / risk parity) reports that after fixing its setup "PPO no longer consistently beats the classical methods out of sample" (weak source, GitHub project). — [GitHub weixunxie](https://github.com/weixunxie/portfolio-optimization-deep-reinforcement-learning)

### Inferences
- Common failure pattern: a handful of years of daily data, a single train/test split, reward = raw return or Sharpe without turnover penalty, many algorithm/hyperparameter trials, and comparison against a cap-weighted index rather than 1/N or the same-universe equal-weight portfolio. Each one inflates backtests; together they explain why the broad SAC test fails.
- Turnover is the decisive failure mode: even where DRL shows timing skill gross, 10 bp costs erase it; in a market with higher round-trip costs the bar is higher still.
- The defensible uses of RL are narrow (execution, cost-aware rebalancing given an external signal) rather than end-to-end portfolio choice; this is an inference, not a tested finding in the sources above.

### Gaps
- No independent study found that re-runs the published FinRL ensemble/DJIA results on a later OOS window; the FinRL papers' own headline Sharpe numbers were not retrieved.
- Winning FinRL Contest agents' OOS performance vs buy-and-hold not retrieved (paper behind 403).
- No live/real-money DRL portfolio track record found.

## 4. Taiwan-specific ML/DL studies (TWSE/TPEx, TEJ-based)

### Takeaway
The best peer-reviewed Taiwan cross-sectional ML study (Bui, Kong, Lin & Lin, Pacific-Basin Finance Journal 2023) finds NN/PLS long-short spreads of 1.20–1.50% per month over 86 anomalies, with stronger predictability among small firms, momentum-type variables important despite Taiwan's weak classic momentum, and shallow nets (2 layers) beating deeper ones. Taiwan is also a test market in recent foundation-model studies (Kronos treats it as out-of-distribution; Rahimikia et al. include it). Most local theses and vendor studies report backtests only, often with implausible returns that signal look-ahead or misalignment errors.

### Cited Findings
- Bui, Kong, Lin & Lin (2023), "Momentum in machine learning: Evidence from the Taiwan stock market," Pacific-Basin Finance Journal, article 102178 (DOI 10.1016/j.pacfin.2023.102178): 86 anomalies; NN and PLS long-short returns **1.20%–1.50% per month**; 5 of the top-20 predictors are momentum-related, notable because Taiwan is one of the few markets without the classic momentum anomaly. — [NYCU Scholar](https://scholar.nycu.edu.tw/en/publications/momentum-in-machine-learning-evidence-from-the-taiwan-stock-marke/)
- Same paper: predictability for small firms is significantly stronger than for big firms across models (authors link this to Taiwanese investors trading small stocks actively); a 2-hidden-layer NN has OOS R² **0.92%**, higher than 3+ layers ("deeper learning does not guarantee better predictive performance"). — [ScienceDirect abstract](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0927538X23002494)
- Kronos includes XTAI as an out-of-distribution test exchange (post-July-2024 test): price IC 0.0448 (Kronos-L) vs 0.0394 for a simple DLinear; volatility MAE 0.0213 vs GARCH 0.0240. — [Kronos arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.02739)
- Rahimikia, Ni & Wang (2025) include Taiwan among 8 OOS test markets (2001–2023); non-US results "broadly consistent" with US (zero-shot TSFMs poor; finance-pretrained better; CatBoost strong). — [arXiv 2511.18578](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.18578v1)
- MDPI Mathematics (2023) 11(23):4758: RF, feed-forward NN, GRU and FinGAT trained on financial ratios of Taiwan stocks; portfolios of top-predicted stocks beat the TW50 benchmark (backtest). — [MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2227-7390/11/23/4758)
- Chen, Leung & Daouk (2003, Computers & Operations Research): probabilistic NN forecasting TAIEX index direction (time-series, not cross-sectional). — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0305054802000370)
- TEJ TQuant Lab (May 2025): ML-built value factor (integrating value and quality) "performs best under a long strategy, with backtest returns significantly above the market" (vendor backtest; also presented at the 2025 Taiwan Finance Association meeting). — [TEJ newsletter](https://www.tejwin.com/news/tej-newsletter-114/); [TEJ empirical studies list](https://eshop.tej.com.tw/E-Shop/journal?category_id=7&subcategory_id=27)
- Local theses illustrate quality problems: an NTU thesis (2020) combining nine algorithms on monthly revenue reports **401.48% annualized** with a 90.90% monthly win rate (backtest); an NSYSU thesis reports a backtest Sharpe ~2x the TAIEX; an NTPU project notes accuracy jumps sharply when predictions are shifted by one day (a classic symptom of label misalignment / look-ahead). — [Airiti (NTU 2020)](https://www.airitilibrary.com/Article/Detail/U0001-3107202020525500); [NSYSU ETD](https://etd.lis.nsysu.edu.tw/ETD-db/ETD-search-c/view_etd?URN=etd-0528123-180654); [NTPU project PDF](https://www.csie.ntpu.edu.tw/uploads/file/project/109/PRJ-NTPUCSIE-109-004.pdf)
- A 2016 NTU thesis used LSTM/CNN on 544 TWSE/TPEx stocks with price-volume, margin balances and three-institutional-investor net buys as inputs. — [NDLTD](https://ndltd.ncl.edu.tw/cgi-bin/gs32/gsweb.cgi/login?o=dnclcdr&s=id%3D%22105NTU05392010%22.&searchmode=basic)

### Inferences
- Taiwan evidence matches the global pattern: modest OOS predictability, concentrated in small stocks, with shallow models sufficient. The 1.20–1.50%/month long-short spreads are gross; with the small-stock concentration and Taiwan's sell-side tax, the long-only, large-cap, net version would be considerably smaller (not quantified in any source found).
- Momentum-type variables matter in ML even though univariate momentum is weak in Taiwan — consistent with nonlinear/interaction effects (e.g., momentum conditioned on volatility or size) rather than a simple linear momentum factor.
- Thesis/vendor results with triple-digit annual returns should be treated as diagnostic of leakage, not as evidence.

### Gaps
- Sample period, value- vs equal-weight split, and transaction-cost results of Bui et al. (2023) not retrieved (full text paywalled).
- Whether Taiwan is in the 46 markets of Cakici et al. (2023) and the 32 EMs of Hanauer & Kalsbach (2023), and its country-level results — not verified.
- No peer-reviewed Taiwan study with explicit TPEx coverage, point-in-time TEJ data and net-of-cost long-only results found.

## 5. Real-world AI-run funds/ETFs as live out-of-sample evidence

### Takeaway
The live record of funds that actually use AI to select stocks is weak: the flagship AIEQ (launched Oct 2017) has roughly half the S&P 500's cumulative return since inception; Qraft's QRFT was liquidated; ESMA found no significant return or alpha difference for EU funds using AI. A momentum-tilted AI ETF (AMOM) is the notable short-window exception. No AI-run fund with a meaningful record was found for Taiwan.

### Cited Findings
- AIEQ (Amplify AI Powered Equity; launched 2017-10-17; 0.75% expense ratio): since inception **+100.2%** vs S&P 500 **≈+199.7%**; YTD 11.90% vs 12.68%; 1-year 16.12% vs 18.34% (article dated 2026-08-26). — [24/7 Wall St., 2026-08-26](https://247wallst.com/investing/2026/08/26/can-ai-help-you-beat-the-stock-market-the-answer-may-surprise-you/)
- AIEQ since-inception average annual return ≈9.86% (snapshot 2026-07-31) / 10.08% (late May 2026). — [StockAnalysis AIEQ](https://stockanalysis.com/etf/aieq/)
- AIEQ lagged the index every year since inception with weaker risk-adjusted results (as of ~2023); later analysis cites turnover of 804% and continued lag vs IVV/QQQM. — [Dividend.com](https://www.dividend.com/active-etfs-channel/how-are-ai-driven-etfs-performing/); [Seeking Alpha](https://seekingalpha.com/article/4949900-aieq-unconvincing-ai-driven-strategy-still-lagging-the-market)
- Early record: AIEQ was up 11.81% in its first year, just edging the S&P 500 — then gave back the outperformance. — [Finadium](https://finadium.com/?p=84847); [stock3](https://stock3.com/news/der-supercomputer-hat-sich-verspekuliert-6532568)
- Other AI-selection ETFs (as of 2026-08-26): AMOM (Qraft AI-Enhanced US Large Cap Momentum) YTD 19.84% / 1-yr 23.50% vs S&P 12.68% / 18.34%; AINT (Finq AI International) YTD 19.68%; AIUP (Finq AI Up) YTD 11.59%; AIVL (WisdomTree US AI Enhanced Value) YTD 8.20% / 1-yr 11.50%; QRFT has been liquidated; several funds are too young for a meaningful record. — [24/7 Wall St.](https://247wallst.com/investing/2026/08/26/can-ai-help-you-beat-the-stock-market-the-answer-may-surprise-you/)
- QRFT 3-year annualized ≈9–10% vs S&P ≈11–12% (window ending mid-2024); 75 bp fee did not deliver sustained outperformance. — [Koala Gains QRFT](https://koalagains.com/etfs/NYSEARCA/QRFT/competition)

- Taiwan: 2026 coverage of Taiwanese active ETFs (e.g., 00981A, 00987A) shows short-window wins and losses vs 0050, but these are human-managed active ETFs, not AI-run; a magazine claim of an "A.I. ETF stock-picking robot" up 90% YTD gave no ticker and could not be verified. — [TechNews, 2026-06-16](https://technews.tw/2026/06/16/looking-only-at-the-volume-content/); [Money Weekly](https://www.moneyweekly.com.tw/_Article?AID=230111)

### Inferences
- The only multi-year live evidence (AIEQ ~9 years; ESMA 3-year cross-section) shows no edge after fees; AIEQ's 804% turnover is the same high-turnover pattern that erodes academic ML spreads.
- AMOM's recent outperformance coincides with a momentum tilt in a momentum-driven market; a 1-year window cannot separate model skill from factor exposure.
- Live results are consistent with the academic finding that gross ML predictability is real but small and fragile after costs, fees and capacity constraints.

### Gaps
- No same-window total-return series (AIEQ vs SPY, with dividends) from a primary data provider; 24/7 Wall St. is a secondary source.
- The US 11-fund study cited by Forbes and ESMA's full tables were not retrieved directly.
- No Taiwan-domiciled fund/ETF found that is explicitly run by an ML stock-selection model with a ≥3-year public record; no SITCA/fund-factsheet comparison retrieved.
